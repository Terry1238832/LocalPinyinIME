Character context statistics
The compact context model contains 1-4 character counts derived from original project-authored prose (MIT) and public-domain original prose by Lu Xun (1881-1936) and Zhu Ziqing (1898-1948).
The full prose and Python authoring dependencies are not included in the installer.
Source titles, authors, links, rights review and checksums: https://github.com/Terry1238832/LocalPinyinIME/blob/main/resources/dictionary/authoring/sources.json
Model transformation and checksums: resources/dictionary/context_zh.metadata.json in the source repository.
Validation works are excluded from training.


Character language model (context_zh.lm)
The 4-gram language model used to rank candidates by the preceding text contains only smoothed character n-gram statistics (no sentences) estimated from:
- the project-authored and public-domain prose listed above;
- Tatoeba Mandarin sentences, https://tatoeba.org, licensed CC BY 2.0 FR (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/fr/), by the Tatoeba contributors;
- Chinese Wikinews (zh.wikinews.org) article dump of 2026-10-01, licensed CC BY 2.5 / CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.5/), by the Wikinews contributors;
- Mozilla Common Voice zh-CN and zh-TW sentence text, released under CC0 1.0 (https://github.com/common-voice/common-voice, commit 7015419eb71f82ac8121f91d3f4c5338b83d6844);
- KdConv dialogue utterances (https://github.com/thu-coai/KdConv, commit 653db76432de09a004ba708a68f8bbd5500e6bec) and
  CrossWOZ dialogue utterances (https://github.com/thu-coai/CrossWOZ, commit df82c9fdff91b9b130f2d6b89110d3870ba6260e),
  both by the Tsinghua University CoAI group and licensed under the Apache License 2.0 (full text above);
  only n-gram statistics derived from the utterances are included, no utterance text;
- FineWeb-2 Mandarin web text (https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-2, data/cmn_Hani/test/000_00000.parquet;
  the word association model also uses every 8th row group of data/cmn_Hani/train/000_00000.parquet),
  by Hugging Face, made available under the Open Data Commons Attribution License v1.0
  (https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/by/1-0/); the underlying pages were collected by Common Crawl
  and are also subject to the Common Crawl terms of use (https://commoncrawl.org/terms-of-use);
  only n-gram statistics are included, no web text;
- LCCC-base real chat text (https://huggingface.co/datasets/silver/lccc, revision 5bd582fa28cd7143f2f9c852e08e23089d677c44,
  labelled MIT there; upstream https://github.com/thu-coai/CDial-GPT), from Wang et al., A Large-Scale Chinese Short-Text
  Conversation Dataset, NLPCC 2020 (arXiv:2008.03946); the underlying posts were collected from Weibo;
  only statistics and network weights derived from it are included, no text;
- internal n-grams of the bundled dictionary words.
The word association model (word_zh.lm) contains only association scores of adjacent lexicon word pairs and runs of
three words, estimated from the same sources. The neural character model (neural_zh.lm) contains only network
weights trained on the same sources; no text is included. The chat phrase counts (phrase_zh.lm) contain only hashes and
rounded counts of short clauses of the LCCC-base text; no text is included.
Text was converted to simplified Chinese with OpenCC (Apache-2.0), which is used at authoring time only.
Sources, downloads and checksums: resources/dictionary/authoring/context_lm_sources.json and resources/dictionary/context_zh.lm.metadata.json in the source repository.
One line in forty and all evaluation sentences are held out of training.


Next-text continuations
The bundled short next-text continuation pairs are original first-party text authored by LocalPinyinIME contributors and distributed under the project MIT license.
Weights are relative editorial preferences. No corpus, commercial lexicon or user conversation history is imported.
Authorship, review and checksums: resources/dictionary/authoring/continuations_sources.json and resources/dictionary/continuations_zh.metadata.json.

English word list (english_words.tsv, 本地英文)
Which spellings are English words comes from SCOWL (Spell Checker Oriented Word
Lists) version 2020.12.07 by Kevin Atkinson, http://wordlist.aspell.net/:
the english, american and special lists up to size 60 (offered), and the
British, Canadian and Australian lists up to size 60 and every list's size 70
words (accepted as typed). Possessives were left out. How often each word is
used was counted from:
- Mozilla Common Voice English sentence text, released under CC0 1.0
  (https://github.com/common-voice/common-voice, commit
  7015419eb71f82ac8121f91d3f4c5338b83d6844);
- Tatoeba English sentences, https://tatoeba.org, licensed CC BY 2.0 FR
  (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/fr/), by the Tatoeba
  contributors (export of 2026-10-17);
- English Wikinews (en.wikinews.org) article dump of 2026-10-01, licensed
  CC BY 2.5 / CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.5/), by
  the Wikinews contributors.
Only word counts are included, no sentence text. Sources and checksums:
resources/dictionary/authoring/english_lexicon_sources.json in the source repository.

The 35 level includes frequency classes 2-6 and words appearing in at
least 11 of 12 dictionaries as indicated in the 12Dicts package.  All
words from the 12Dicts package have had likely inflections added via
my inflection database.

The 40 level includes words from Alan's 3esl list found in version 4.0
of his 12dicts package.  Like his other stuff the 3esl list is also in the
public domain.

The 50 level includes Brian's frequency class 1, words appearing
in at least 5 of 12 of the dictionaries as indicated in the 12Dicts
package, and uppercase words in at least 4 of the previous 12
dictionaries.  A decent number of proper names is also included: The
top 1000 male, female, and Last names from the 1990 Census report; a
list of names sent to me by Alan Beale; and a few names that I added
myself.  Finally a small list of abbreviations not commonly found in
other word lists is included.

The name files form the Census report is a government document which I
don't think can be copyrighted.

The file special-jargon.50 uses common.lst and word.lst from the
"Unofficial Jargon File Word Lists" which is derived from "The Jargon
File".  All of which is in the Public Domain.  This file also contain
a few extra UNIX terms which are found in the file "unix-terms" in the
special/ directory.

The 55 level includes words from Alan's 2of4brif list found in version
4.0 of his 12dicts package.  Like his other stuff the 2of4brif is also
in the public domain.

The 60 level includes all words appearing in at least 2 of the 12
dictionaries as indicated by the 12Dicts package.

The 70 level includes Brian's frequency class 0 and the 74,550 common
dictionary words from the MWords package.  The common dictionary words,
like those from the 12Dicts package, have had all likely inflections
added.  The 70 level also included the 5desk list from version 4.0 of
the 12Dics package which is in the public domain.

The 80 level includes the ENABLE word list, all the lists in the
ENABLE supplement package (except for ABLE), the "UK Advanced Cryptics
Dictionary" (UKACD), the list of signature words from the YAWL package,
and the 10,196 places list from the MWords package.

The 95 level includes the 354,984 single words, 256,772 compound
words, 4,946 female names and the 3,897 male names, and 21,986 names
from the MWords package, ABLE.LST from the ENABLE Supplement, and some
additional words found in my part-of-speech database that were not
found anywhere else.

Accent information was taken from UKACD.
